## Detailed Opportunity Cards

### Deterministic Workflow

---

**1. Tax Document Gathering (US/UK)**

**Category:** Deterministic Workflow

**Why it's a good candidate:**  
Clear goal (all tax docs in one place), repeatable each year, many known sources (banks, brokers, employers, etc.). Good fit for checklists, templates, and optional automation (e.g. linking to accounts or parsing statements).

**Current pain point:**  
You have to hunt across many international and local accounts to find and download the right documents. It’s time-consuming and easy to miss something, especially with dual US/UK rules.

**How AI helps:**  
- A **checklist** of document types and typical sources (US/UK) that you tick off as you gather.  
- Optional: **AI-assisted parsing** of statements or PDFs to extract key numbers and suggest which document is which.  
- One **folder or doc template** (e.g. in Notion or Drive) where you (or a script) place files by category (income, investments, property, etc.) so nothing is forgotten.

**Getting started:**  
This week: list every account (UK and US) that might produce tax-relevant documents. Create a simple checklist (spreadsheet or Notion) with one row per document type and a column for “where I get this.” Use that as the backbone for next year’s gathering.

### Collaborative AI

---

**2. Travel Itinerary from Multi-Source Research**

**Category:** Collaborative AI

**Why it's a good candidate:**  
You’re already pulling from many sources (TikTok, Instagram, Reddit, Google, flight tools). That’s language- and content-heavy work that AI can summarize and structure. You stay in the driver’s seat (dates, preferences, budget) while AI does the aggregation and first draft.

**Current pain point:**  
Building a trip means manually scraping and reading content from social media, forums, search, and flight sites. It takes a lot of time to decide “the best thing” and turn it into a coherent itinerary.

**How AI helps:**  
- You provide: destination, dates, interests, and (optionally) links or pasted snippets from TikTok, Reddit, Instagram, or articles.  
- AI summarizes recommendations, highlights recurring “must-dos,” and drafts a day-by-day itinerary with options.  
- You edit and approve; AI can refine based on your feedback (e.g. “more food, less museums”) or add practical details (opening hours, booking links) where you paste them in.

**Getting started:**  
This week: for one upcoming trip, paste 5–10 saved posts or links (Reddit threads, a few TikToks or articles) into a single doc and ask an AI to extract “top places/activities” and one suggested day order. Use that as a first-draft itinerary and adjust by hand. That becomes the pattern for a reusable workflow.

**Category:** Collaborative AI

**Why it's a good candidate:**  
You’re already saving things; the gap is organization and follow-through. AI can help structure saved items (event name, link, sign-up steps, free/paid, dates) and suggest when to slot them into your calendar based on when you’re free.

### Autonomous Agent

---

**4. Weekly Investment Brief**

**Category:** Autonomous Agent

**Why it's a good candidate:**  
You want a recurring, structured output (weekly brief) that depends on multiple data sources (market performance, macro/politics, stock ideas) and benefits from reasoning over time (week-over-week). That fits an agent that runs on a schedule, pulls data and news, and produces a consistent brief for you to review.

**Current pain point:**  
Staying on top of markets, global economics, and politics, and turning that into “what to look at” and “top stocks for the week” is time-consuming. Doing it week over week in a structured way is hard to sustain manually.

**How AI helps:**  
- **Trigger:** Every week (e.g. Sunday evening or Monday morning).  
- **Agent:** Fetches market performance (indices, your portfolio if you connect it), scans headlines and summaries for global economics and politics, and optionally incorporates your criteria (e.g. sectors, risk).  
- **Output:** A short brief: (1) how the market did, (2) what’s worth watching (economics/politics), (3) top stock ideas or themes for the week, (4) optional week-over-week comparison so you can build a narrative over time.  
- You read and decide; the agent doesn’t trade, it informs.

**Getting started:**  
This week: define the exact sections you want in the brief (e.g. “Market snapshot,” “Macro/politics,” “Stocks to watch,” “Week over week”). Manually produce one such brief for the past week using free sources (e.g. Yahoo Finance, BBC/Reuters). That template becomes the spec for the automated workflow.

## Appendix: Category Definitions

- **Deterministic Workflow**: A repeatable process with clear inputs, rules, and outputs that AI can execute reliably with minimal supervision. Examples: formatting reports, processing forms, generating routine communications, data transformation.

- **Collaborative AI**: Human and AI work together in real time. The human drives the process; AI contributes suggestions, drafts, analysis, or feedback. Examples: co-writing, brainstorming, code review, data analysis.

- **Autonomous Agent**: A goal-driven workflow where AI plans and executes steps autonomously. The agent reasons about what to do, calls tools as needed, and adapts its approach. Ranges from a single agent handling a complex task to multi-agent systems. Examples: competitor monitoring, research → analysis → report pipelines, intake → triage → routing.
